How it works

From a 402 to a receipt that outlives your database.

Five steps, all machine-to-machine. You configure it once; every payment after that documents itself.

  1. The agent hits a paywall

    A paid endpoint answers with 402 Payment Required and an x402 quote.

  2. Payment settles

    The agent pays in USDC on Base. The facilitator verifies and settles the transfer.

  3. Traceipt verifies, then issues

    It confirms the on-chain transfer before signing — binding the payment to what was bought, the payer, and the payee. No phantom receipts.

  4. The receipt is chained & anchored

    Each receipt joins a tamper-evident hash chain and is folded into an on-chain Merkle anchor.

  5. Anyone verifies — forever

    Signature, chain, and anchor all check offline against published keys. The receipt outlives the service that issued it.

Built for what's arriving

Autonomous spending is landing at the same moment regulators want it logged, traceable, and provable. Traceipt produces exactly that record.

EU AI Act · Article 12

Automatic, traceable logs

High-risk AI systems must automatically record events over their lifetime, with enough traceability to reconstruct what happened. A signed receipt per payment, permanently verifiable, is that record for an agent's spending.

MiCA · record-keeping

Transactions linked to settlement

Records must tie a commercial event to its executed transaction, kept for years and usable by supervisors. Each Traceipt binds quote, resource, and on-chain settlement in one signed, sequential object.

Traceipt provides the technical record these rules require — it is not legal advice, and whether a given deployment is in scope is a determination for counsel. The honest mapping and its limits are documented in full, including exactly what the service does not claim.

What you get

A record you — and a stranger — can trust.

Cryptographic signatures

Every receipt is Ed25519-signed and verifiable offline in any language via published JWKS — no SDK, no callback to us.

Verified before issued

The on-chain transfer is checked against the chain first. A receipt only exists for a payment that actually happened.

Tamper-evident ledger

A per-seller hash chain with sequential numbering; signature-checked end to end, so a rewritten database is caught, not trusted.

Merkle anchoring

Periodic RFC 6962 roots published on-chain prove a receipt existed by a given time — provable without ever trusting our database.

Fraud-resistant by design

Payer and payee are cryptographically bound — nobody can mint a receipt for a payment they didn't make or receive.

Accountant-ready invoices

A VAT-aware A4 PDF for every receipt, with a scan-to-verify QR — the artifact a human files, backed by the record a machine checks.
